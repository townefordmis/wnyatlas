## Per-site findings

### Magnus / Former NL Industries Foundry

DEC's 2005 decision traces Buffalo Brass operations from 1892, Magnus Metal from 1899, early Empire Smelting operations, and National Lead ownership from 1936 to 1972. Investigations documented lead-bearing soil and fill. DEC's December 2009 notice reports remedial construction completed in September 2008. Excavated soil was consolidated in an on-site containment cell and capped; clean fill, fencing and monitoring were also installed. The remedy retains contamination and requires site management and industrial/commercial restrictions. The document archive contains later periodic reviews; their listing alone does not verify current compliance. The historical 779 Walden entry remains an unresolved address match.

### Michael Heyman / East Ferry Street

The former 3.32-acre zinc and lead smelting and refining property contained lead in ash and fill. DEC's 2001 and 2004 investigations found contamination extending west onto additional industrial and commercial properties. DEC reports excavation of 136,234 tons of lead-contaminated soil and completion of cleanup work in January 2008. These findings describe the investigated properties and historical remedy. They do not establish exposure throughout the neighborhood or unrestricted use of every parcel today.

Sources: https://dec.ny.gov/environmental-protection/site-cleanup/regional-remediation-project-information/region-9/der-success-stories

### Lumen Bearing

EPA reviewed historical trade publications, directories, aerial photographs, and Sanborn maps before investigating this former foundry/smelter in October and November 2014. The initial samples included lead up to 20,000 parts per million at depths of six to eighteen inches. EPA installed fencing and erosion controls in 2015. Its final report documents completion on July 18, 2016 of a geotextile and approximately three-inch crushed-stone cover across the site to reduce direct contact and migration. This was stabilization of contaminated soil in place, not removal of all lead-bearing soil or an unrestricted-use determination. The September 2016 final report does not establish the cover's present condition.

Sources: https://response.epa.gov/site/site_profile.aspx?site_id=11718 | https://response.epa.gov/site/sitrep_profile.aspx?site_id=11718&counter=27353

### Lake Erie Smelting Corporation

EPA's April 2020 report places the former secondary lead smelter within the later Towne Gardens apartment complex. Superior Street had been removed during redevelopment; the historical factory footprint was occupied by apartment buildings. The report documents a contaminated-soil removal that mobilized in September 2019 and resumed excavation in April 2020 under EPA oversight. This report documents work in progress, not a final completion finding or current conditions. The separately documented move to 127 Fillmore does not extend the Superior Street sampling results to that address.

### Cornell / Niagara White Lead

   The 1859 directory lists Niagara White Lead at Delaware and Virginia. The 1887 Industries of Buffalo identifies Cornell Lead's office and works at that corner and names white lead, lead pipe, sheet lead, and bar lead as its products. These period records establish lead manufacturing at the intersection. They do not establish environmental sampling or a remedy for the nineteenth-century factory footprint. Reconstruct the factory parcels, later land uses, and address-specific investigation and cleanup records. A later Cornell or National Lead listing at another address cannot settle the history of this footprint.
