# WNYAtlas Site-History Expansion Audit

Last reviewed: August 12, 2026

## Audit result

- 175 mapped place records were reviewed by the existing history-depth audit.
- All 175 have a structured story.
- All 175 meet the current minimum depth standard of 15 story elements.
- All 175 have at least two listed sources.
- Six places currently have dedicated historical-aerial comparisons: Republic Steel, Bethlehem Steel, Tonawanda Coke, Hyde Park Landfill, 102nd Street Landfill, and S-Area Landfill.
- Love Canal, Buffalo River, and Bethlehem Steel currently demonstrate the deeper feature-chapter approach: a significant event or human story is explained with its own visual hierarchy, scope notes, and direct sources.

The next need is therefore not bulk page creation. It is selective historical strengthening: identify the event, people, process, or landscape change that makes each place understandable, then document it without turning every record into an oversized page.

## Scalable publishing model

### Tier 1 — Flagship feature chapter

Use for regionally important sites with a well-documented defining story, strong primary or institutional sources, and enough evidence to support a distinct narrative section. These can receive a Buffalo River- or Bethlehem-style chapter.

Required elements:

1. A clearly named historical question or event.
2. Exact dates or an explicitly bounded date range.
3. Named companies, agencies, workers, communities, or responsible parties when the record supports attribution.
4. A plain-language explanation of the industrial process or waste pathway.
5. Documented human, environmental, labor, or community consequences without unsupported health causation.
6. Government response, enforcement, cleanup decision, or remedy.
7. A scope note explaining what the evidence does not establish.
8. At least two strong sources, with primary government, archival, or contemporary records preferred.

### Tier 2 — Expanded standard history

Use for sites with meaningful history but not enough evidence or distinct material for a separate feature chapter. Strengthen the existing background, timeline, impacts, cleanup, and present-day sections while retaining the shared page layout.

### Tier 3 — Research hold

Use when records establish that a site exists but do not yet support a fuller public narrative. Preserve concise verified facts and a research question. Do not pad the page with generic industrial history or inference.

## Editorial rules

- Do not equate a facility, spill, fire, explosion, disposal record, or contaminant detection with proven individual exposure or illness.
- Do not turn an incomplete casualty tally into a complete plant-wide total.
- Distinguish predecessor companies from later owners and corporate successors.
- Distinguish the historical facility footprint from current cleanup, parcel, easement, plume, and exposure boundaries.
- Preserve uncertainty in dates, quantities, and locations rather than resolving conflicts silently.
- Name workers and community members when reliable records support it and when doing so adds understanding rather than spectacle.
- Use archival aerials to show physical change, not to infer contamination that sampling records do not establish.
- Keep the most consequential fact near the beginning; move technical remedy detail lower on the page.

## Recommended implementation

Create one reusable, data-driven historical-feature format for dates, key figures, named events, scope notes, and sources. Use custom page components only when a place truly needs a unique visual treatment. This keeps the Atlas consistent and makes expansion to every documented place maintainable.

The first production batch should be Tonawanda Coke, Republic Steel, Hyde Park, 102nd Street, and S-Area. After that batch is reviewed live, apply the refined format to the remaining priority groups.
